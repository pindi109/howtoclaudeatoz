# Running Meetings More Efficiently with Claude (2026 Guide)

*Last updated: 2026-06-07*

Claude for meetings addresses one of the most consistent time drains in business: the preparation before meetings and the admin after them. Claude can write agendas, generate discussion frameworks, transform rough notes into structured summaries, pull out action items with owners and deadlines, and draft follow-up emails. Used consistently, it can save 30–60 minutes per meeting in combined preparation and admin time. This guide covers each stage of the meeting lifecycle with the prompts that produce the best results.

## How Can Claude Help Your Business with Meetings?

Claude's meeting value comes in three stages:

- **Pre-meeting:** Agenda creation, briefing documents, background research summaries
- **Post-meeting:** Note processing, action item extraction, follow-up emails
- **Supporting materials:** Decision logs, meeting minutes, board pack preparation

Claude does not attend or record meetings. For transcription, you need a tool like Otter.ai, Fireflies, or your video platform's native transcription. Claude then processes the transcript.

## How to Use Claude for Meetings — Step by Step

### Step 1: Write a Meeting Agenda

Provide Claude with the meeting's purpose, attendees, duration, and the topics to cover.

Prompt:
> "Write a meeting agenda for the following. Meeting purpose: [describe]. Attendees: [list roles, not names necessary]. Duration: [e.g. 60 minutes]. Topics to cover: [list]. Include: time allocation for each item, a clear objective for each discussion point, and a 'before the meeting' preparation note for attendees. Format as a clean, shareable document."

### Step 2: Prepare a Pre-Meeting Briefing

For complex or important meetings, Claude can produce a briefing document:

> "Write a pre-meeting briefing for [meeting type and purpose]. Include: background context ([paste relevant notes]), the decision or outcome we need to reach, the key questions that need answering, and any potential points of disagreement to anticipate. Keep it to one page."

### Step 3: Set Up a Note-Taking Template

Before the meeting, Claude can create a structured note-taking template based on the agenda:

> "Using the agenda above, create a note-taking template I can use during the meeting. For each agenda item, include a space for: discussion summary, decisions made, action items (with owner and deadline). Format for easy filling in during the meeting."

### Step 4: Process Meeting Notes or Transcript

After the meeting, paste your notes or transcript and ask Claude to process them:

**For rough notes:**
> "I'm going to paste rough notes from a meeting. Please: (1) write a clear 3–5 sentence summary of what was discussed and decided, (2) extract all action items and format them as a table with columns: Action, Owner, Deadline, (3) list any open questions that were raised but not resolved. Notes: [paste]"

**For a transcript:**
> "Here is a transcript from a [meeting type] meeting. Please: (1) summarise the key discussion points and decisions in plain language, (2) identify all commitments and action items mentioned, with who owns each one, (3) note any agenda items that were not reached or need to be deferred. Transcript: [paste]"

### Step 5: Extract and Format Action Items

If you want just the action items processed quickly:

> "From the notes/transcript below, extract every action item, commitment, or follow-up mentioned. Format as a table: Action | Person Responsible | Due Date. If a date or owner was not specified, mark as TBC. Notes: [paste]"

### Step 6: Draft the Follow-Up Email

> "Write a follow-up email to send to [meeting attendees / client / team] after today's [meeting type]. Include: brief thank-you or acknowledgement of the meeting, 3–4 bullet summary of what was agreed, action items list (formatted clearly), next meeting date if applicable. Tone: [professional/friendly]. From: [sender name/role]."

### Step 7: Write Formal Meeting Minutes

For board meetings, shareholder meetings, or any meeting requiring a formal record:

> "Write formal meeting minutes from the notes below. Include: meeting title, date, attendees, apologies, items discussed (with brief summary of discussion and decision for each), action items, date of next meeting. Notes: [paste]"

## Meeting Types — Claude Prompts by Format

| Meeting Type | Claude's Most Useful Role | Key Prompt Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly team standup | Post-meeting action log | Extract blockers and actions |
| Client project update | Follow-up email + decision log | Professional summary, clear actions |
| Board / investor meeting | Formal minutes + board pack prep | Formal structure, decision record |
| Sales call debrief | Next steps email + CRM note | Agreed next steps, client requirements noted |
| Strategic planning session | Ideas capture + decision summary | Themes, decisions, and parking lot items |
| One-to-one (manager/employee) | Notes into structured record | Topics covered, agreed actions, development points |
| Kick-off meeting | Project brief from notes | Scope, responsibilities, timeline, risks |

## Claude for Meetings — Time Savings Breakdown

| Task | Manual Time | With Claude | Time Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agenda writing | 15–25 min | 2–5 min | 10–20 min |
| Pre-meeting briefing | 30–60 min | 5–10 min | 25–50 min |
| Notes to action items | 20–30 min | 3–5 min | 15–25 min |
| Follow-up email | 10–20 min | 2–3 min | 8–17 min |
| Formal minutes | 45–90 min | 10–15 min | 35–75 min |

For a business running 10 meetings per week, consistent Claude use on agendas and follow-ups alone can save 3–5 hours weekly.

## Tips for Better Claude Meeting Outputs

- Capture rough notes during the meeting, even in shorthand — Claude is good at interpreting abbreviated notes
- Include decisions explicitly in your notes ("decided to...") — Claude extracts these most reliably when they're marked
- Name action item owners in your notes — Claude cannot invent names that weren't mentioned
- For long transcripts, break them into sections (before the main discussion, during, and after) to help Claude process them more accurately
- Always review action items before sending — Claude occasionally merges or splits items that should be separate
